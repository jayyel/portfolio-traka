Today's lesson

Today's tape is a case study in the difference between 'sentiment recovery' and 'leverage recovery' — and why conflating them destroys basis traders. The Fear & Greed index moved from 25 (Extreme Fear) to 29 (Fear) while BTC spot gained +1.54% and ETF inflows hit $137.6M, yet perp funding remained near-neutral rather than spiking to the 10–20%+ annualized levels seen in prior bull runs. [STRONG — sentiment vs funding divergence is observable] That gap — strong spot demand with muted derivatives leverage — is what keeps the cash-and-carry basis trade healthy: when ETF buyers dominate, they push up spot prices and create contango on CME futures without simultaneously pushing up funding on perps, so the arb spread stays wide and you're not fighting against crowded longs on both legs. [STRONG — mechanistic] The lesson flips dangerously when retail starts chasing: in prior cycles, by the time Fear & Greed crossed 60 (Greed), perp funding was already above 30% annualized, compressing basis to near zero and making the carry trade uneconomical — the moment you need to enter is the moment the crowd has already closed the spread. [LIKELY — historical pattern] The actionable inference: your window to reload or extend basis trade duration at attractive carry levels is likely now, when sentiment is still in Fear and institutional flows are the primary driver, rather than after a sentiment surge forces you to compete with momentum capital.
